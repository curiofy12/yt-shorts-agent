# YouTube Shorts Growth Report
**Sample:** 20 videos | **Period:** Oct 19–26, 2026
**Caveat:** With 20 videos and most pulling under 100 views, this sample is too small to draw statistically confident conclusions. The patterns below are directional signals, not proven rules. Three videos have 0 views and may simply not have been indexed yet — exclude them from retention analysis.

---

## 1. What's Working

**Top performers by views:**
| Video | Topic | Views | Avg Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fire-setting bird | Animal behavior | 1,000 | 49s |
| Drifting ship | Unsolved mystery | 709 | 72s |
| Pistol shrimp | Animal behavior | 161 | 73s |
| Space/time | Mind-bending space | 89 | 70s |
| 1983 man saved world | Historical near-miss | 49 | 48s |

**Three clear patterns emerge:**

**A) Animal behavior topics are outperforming everything else.**
The two animal videos (pistol shrimp: 161 views / 73s retention; fire bird: 1,000 views / 49s retention) account for the majority of total views. These aren't just high-view — the shrimp video's 73-second average duration is the second-highest in the dataset, suggesting people are actually finishing or rewatching it.

**B) High average view duration correlates loosely with mystery/sensory hooks.**
The top 5 videos by avg duration are: Fibonacci/math pattern (102s — though only 18 views, so low confidence), pistol shrimp (73s), drifting ship (72s), mantis shrimp color (72s), and space/time (70s). The common thread: hooks that open an unexplained gap ("how is this possible?") rather than framing the video as a history lesson.

**C) The drifting ship hook is the clearest example of what's working structurally.**
709 views with a 72s avg duration. The hook front-loads a physical, visceral image ("hot food, no people") with zero setup needed. It creates immediate tension. Compare to the Boston Tea Party (34 views, 45s): "You've been lied to" is a common, worn-out format and the topic requires prior investment to care.

**D) ~20:00 UTC posting time slightly outperforms ~22:00 UTC on view counts** for comparable topics, though the sample is far too small to treat this as reliable.

## 2. What's Underperforming

**Bottom performers:**
| Video | Topic | Views | Avg Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| They laughed / Nobel (generic) | Ridiculed scientist | 0 | 0s |
| Language/stolen words | Language fact | 0 | 0s |
| Golden Ratio (repeat) | Math in nature | 0 | 0s |
| Internet ocean cables | Tech infrastructure | 6 | 37s |
| Mantis shrimp colors | Animal ability | 5 | 72s |

**Specific problems:**

**A) "Ridiculed scientist" is a played-out format — and you've posted it three times.**
Three videos use this template (Barry Marshall/bacteria: 14 views; handwashing doctor: 12 views; generic "They laughed" Nobel: 0 views). Retention on these averages ~32s. The third one getting 0 views likely reflects the algorithm not distributing it *and* a hook ("They laughed at him. Then he won the Nobel Prize.") that is nearly word-for-word identical to the first two. This topic category appears exhausted for now.

**B) Benjamin Franklin "secret side hustle" had 53 views but only 21s avg duration — the worst retention of any video with meaningful reach.**
People clicked, then left almost immediately. The hook over-promised ("secret business empire") on a topic that may not have delivered fast enough. High click, low watch = the algorithm likely suppressed it after early signals.

**C) The Golden Ratio topic was posted twice in four days (Oct 24 and Oct 26)** with near-identical hooks. The second got 0 views. Posting duplicate-adjacent content in the same week is likely hurting distribution.

**D) Technology/infrastructure topics are consistently weak:** internet cables (6 views), 1962 internet prediction (9 views), ancient steel (32 views). Abstract or "did you know" facts without a human character or visual tension appear to underperform.
